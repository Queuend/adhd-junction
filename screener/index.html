Official external resource

Adult ADHD Self-Report Screener ASRS v1.1

On the NYU page, open the English patient-facing six-question screener. Its shaded blocks show the screening ranges. A result can suggest that further investigation may be worthwhile, but it cannot diagnose ADHD or rule it out.

ABOUT THE INSTRUMENT

Use the result as a prompt, not a verdict.

The six-question Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale v1.1 Screener is Part A of the longer 18-question checklist. The authorised patient-facing version includes its own shaded scoring ranges. It is the ASRS v1.1, not the separate, later ASRS-5.

A screening result does not prove ADHD, measure its severity or explain what is causing a difficulty. A result below the screening threshold does not rule ADHD out.

Instrument and source

The 6-question Adult Self-Report Scale-Version1.1 (ASRS-V1.1) Screener is a subset of the 18-question Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale-Version1.1 (Adult ASRSV1.1) Symptom Checklist. © New York University and the President and Fellows of Harvard College.

Developed by Lenard Adler, MD (New York University), and Ronald C. Kessler, PhD (Harvard). The NYU licensing page supplies the authorised patient-facing version, attribution and conditions of use.

NYU: instrument, conditions of use and patient-facing version
